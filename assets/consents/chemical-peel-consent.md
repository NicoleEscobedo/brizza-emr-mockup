## What this form is

This form is your informed consent for a chemical peel at Brizza Aesthetics. Written approval of your treatment plan and estimated cost is a **separate** step. Signing this form does not replace that approval.


## The procedure and who performs it

A chemical peel uses a chemical solution on the skin to exfoliate or remove outer layers. The goal is to improve the look of the skin, such as texture or tone. The official appointment type is **Chemical Peel**. The clinic SOPs refer to a selected peel type, but they do not name official peel products or strengths (SOP 03 §6 and §9).

A licensed **Aesthetician** performs this treatment and obtains your informed consent as the treating provider for this service. A Medical Assistant may help with support tasks and may present or witness this form, but the MA does not obtain your informed consent and does not perform the peel.

**Sample clinical content (not in the clinic SOPs; for this fictional project only).** Sample peel types for this fictional project: **superficial** peels such as glycolic, lactic, salicylic, or Jessner's. Medium-depth TCA and deeper peels are **not** listed, because this service is performed by the Aesthetician and SOP 03 does not authorize a medium-depth protocol. ## Expected results

Results vary from person to person. Some people see an improvement in skin texture, tone, or brightness after healing. The skin often looks red, flaky, or peeling during recovery. More than one treatment may be recommended. Results are not permanent. **No result is guaranteed.** This treatment will not fix every concern.

**Sample clinical content (not in the clinic SOPs; for this fictional project only).** For a superficial peel, redness, tightness, and flaking are commonly noticeable for about **3 to 7 days** [SAMPLE]. A series of **3 to 6 treatments**, often spaced about **2 to 4 weeks** apart, is commonly recommended [SAMPLE]. A deeper or unexpected peel can mean a longer recovery.

## Common risks

Common risks include, but are not limited to:

- Redness, stinging, burning, tightness, swelling, or peeling
- Dryness or flaking
- Temporary darkening or lightening of the skin
- Sensitivity to the sun
- Breakouts
- Results that are less than, or different from, what you hoped for

## Rare but serious risks

Rare but serious risks include, but are not limited to:

- Scarring
- Lasting **hyperpigmentation** or hypopigmentation (darker or lighter patches)
- Infection
- **Cold sore (herpes) reactivation**
- Allergic or chemical burn reaction
- A peel that is deeper than expected, with a longer or more difficult recovery
- Eye injury if solution enters the eyes [SAMPLE]
- Prolonged redness [SAMPLE]
- Milia (small white bumps) [SAMPLE]
- A visible line between treated and untreated skin [SAMPLE]

The combined list above is a sample final rare-risk list for a superficial peel in this fictional project, pending Medical Director review. Risks can vary with peel depth or product. [SAMPLE]

## Who should not have this treatment

Tell your provider if any of the following apply to you. Your provider reviews your history and decides whether treatment can proceed. This list is not a complete medical evaluation. Additional screening items below are sample items for this fictional project. [SAMPLE]

Check any that apply, or check “None of these”:

- ☐ I am pregnant, think I might be pregnant, or am trying to become pregnant
- ☐ I am breastfeeding
- ☐ I have an active infection, cold sore, open wound, or rash in an area to be treated
- ☐ I am using or have used isotretinoin (Accutane or a similar medicine) in the past **6 months** [SAMPLE]
- ☐ I have a history of abnormal scarring (including keloids) or of lasting pigment change after injury or procedures
- ☐ I have a known allergy to a planned peel ingredient
- ☐ I have had a recent sunburn, used a tanning bed, or had waxing, laser, or another facial procedure in an area to be treated [SAMPLE]
- ☐ I am using a prescription retinoid, or a strong acid or exfoliant, and I have not discussed this with my provider [SAMPLE]
- ☐ I have a history of cold sores (herpes) in or near an area to be treated [SAMPLE]
- ☐ I have a current flare of eczema, psoriasis, rosacea, or dermatitis in an area to be treated [SAMPLE]
- ☐ None of these

## Alternatives

Alternatives include doing nothing, using a home skin-care plan only, choosing a milder or different peel if one is appropriate, or considering other clinic services your provider discusses with you, such as a customized facial or microneedling if those are appropriate. You are not required to have this treatment.

## Pre-care acknowledgments

I understand that I should complete or update my medical history, medications, allergies, and current skin-care routine before treatment, and that I must tell my provider about any changes. I will follow any pre-treatment instructions the clinic gives me.

**Sample clinical content (not in the clinic SOPs; for this fictional project only).** Use these sample pre-care steps unless your provider gives you different written instructions:

- Arrive with clean skin. Avoid makeup on the area to be treated, if you can.
- For about **5 to 7 days** before treatment, pause retinoids, AHAs/BHAs, benzoyl peroxide, scrubs, and other strong exfoliants unless your provider says otherwise. [SAMPLE]
- Avoid sunburn, tanning beds, waxing, and other facial procedures in the treatment area for the period your provider gives you.
- If you have a history of cold sores, tell your provider. An antiviral medicine may be recommended.
- Do not use isotretinoin within **6 months** before a peel unless your provider specifically clears you. [SAMPLE]
- Tell your provider about all medicines, allergies, and any change in your health, including pregnancy or breastfeeding.

A provider-approved clinic instruction sheet, when one is issued, replaces this sample list.

## Post-care acknowledgments

I will receive verbal and written aftercare instructions that are specific to the peel performed. I will receive a printed or electronic copy. I will follow those instructions. I know how to contact the clinic with questions or concerns. Clinical questions go to my treating provider (the Aesthetician). I understand the clinic sends a post-treatment check-in message after this service, and that this message is separate from any formal follow-up visit. A follow-up visit is scheduled only if my provider recommends one.

**Sample clinical content (not in the clinic SOPs; for this fictional project only).** Use these sample aftercare steps unless the written sheet for the peel you received says otherwise:

- Keep the area clean. Use only the bland cleanser and moisturizer your provider approves. Do not pick, peel, or scrub flaking skin.
- Use a broad-spectrum sunscreen (SPF 30 or higher) every day. Avoid direct sun, tanning beds, and heat (sauna, steam, or strenuous exercise) while the skin is healing, commonly the first several days. [SAMPLE]
- Do not restart retinoids, acids, or exfoliants until your provider says the skin has healed, often about **5 to 7 days**. [SAMPLE]
- Do not wax or use other facial procedures on the treated area until you are cleared.
- Call the clinic if you have increasing pain, spreading redness, pus, a fever, blistering that worries you, or a peel that seems much deeper than you were told to expect. For trouble breathing or a severe allergic reaction, seek emergency care.

A provider-approved clinic instruction sheet, when one is issued, replaces this sample list.

## Right to refuse or withdraw
