Every form in this set includes a "When you sign this form again" section. The patient re-signs at least once every 12 months, and whenever the service, product, or device changes. For the marketing photo authorization, that re-sign rule and the authorization's own expiration date or event both apply.

## MA-versus-provider signing rule

Clinical informed consent and clinical evaluation remain the **treating provider's** responsibility (Patient Journey SOP §8; service workflows). The Coordinator may collect and route administrative forms. The Medical Assistant may provide authorized clinical support and may assist with permitted documentation, and must not independently alter provider-authored notes.

The SOPs do not contain a word-for-word Medical Assistant witness script. The signature block on these forms therefore uses this project rule, which is consistent with the SOP assignment of informed consent to the treating provider:

> The MA may present this form and witness the patient's signature only. The MA does not obtain informed consent and does not sign as provider.

The treating provider who obtains consent and performs the service is the one who signs as provider:

- **NP Injector** (a licensed nurse practitioner) for Neurotoxin and Filler
- **Aesthetician** for Chemical Peel, Microneedling, and Customized Facials (Mini Facial, Facial, Hydrafacial)
- **Owner/MD** (Dr. Valentina A. Rossi) only when that role is actually treating, which is limited in the SOPs, and as the only performer for Dissolve

## Review flags

A licensed attorney and the medical director must still review these forms before any real-world use. The following items remain open after the 2026-11-27 decisions:

1. **New York scope of practice** — who may inject neuromodulators and fillers; who may perform chemical peels and microneedling (including depth and peel strength); and who may legally obtain informed consent for each service. The SOPs name the Aesthetician as treating provider for peels, microneedling, and facials, the NP Injector for toxin and filler, and the Owner/MD only for Dissolve. That operational assignment is not a legal opinion.
2. **Marketing authorization requirements** — federal HIPAA-style authorization elements and any New York publicity, advertising, or health-information rules; approved channels beyond the sourced Instagram-first emphasis; written-revocation method; whether “until I revoke in writing” is an acceptable expiration event; and any compensation practice.
3. **Fee enforceability, exceptions, and collection** — whether the $50 late-cancel and $50 no-show fees are enforceable as written under New York law; how fees are collected; and any late-cancel exceptions. The 24-hour cutoff and both $50 amounts are settled under SOP 00. Exceptions and collection method remain marked [confirm with Clinic Ops Lead] and are not invented.
4. **Sample clinical content** — peel types, the microneedling device, facial enhancements, result and healing ranges, pre- and post-care, and completed risk and contraindication lists were filled as [SAMPLE] content for this fictional project because the SOPs expressly do not supply clinical protocols. A medical director must approve or replace that content. Official catalogs remain unsourced.
